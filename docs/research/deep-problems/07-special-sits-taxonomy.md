# 07 - Special situations and opportunistic financing: sourced taxonomy

Written 2026-10-23 by a research agent for the Stochastic team.
Scope: how practitioners divide the field, 10 categories with real dated examples, what an AI agent does in each, and a ranking for a 36-hour demo.
Does not repeat the Buyers Edge facts, the Morgan Stanley Tactical Value facts, or the instrument pricing table in `../research.md`.

### Where sources disagree, and corrections to the lead agent's draft

1. The draft's split into "borrower-side triggers" and "investor-side solutions" mixes two axes.
   Firms divide by investor strategy (Oaktree, King Street, Davidson Kempner).
   Law firms divide by transaction structure (Simpson Thacher).
   Neither uses the borrower trigger list.
   Inferred: keep the trigger as the intake label and the structure as the output label.
2. The biggest correction: practitioners separate proactive "capital solutions" for healthy companies from reactive stressed or distressed work.
   KKR renamed its practice from Special Situations (distressed) to Capital Solutions (high-quality businesses).
   Oaktree says it often partners with healthy growing businesses.
   King Street and Apollo put rescue financing inside the same umbrella as capital solutions, so firms are not consistent.
   Our pitch ("needs more than a bank will lend") is the healthy-side capital solutions case.
3. Liability management (amend and extend, uptier, drop-down) is a borrower action, not an investor solution.
   Law firms list it as its own structure alongside DIP and distressed M&A, and Macfarlanes files it under distressed.
   Amend and extend and covenant waivers are a milder, bank-side relative that shows up in ordinary 8-Ks.
   I split them into two categories below (C4 and C5).
4. Event-driven strategies (merger arbitrage, activism, spin-off investing) are not financing.
   Davidson Kempner lists Merger Arbitrage as its own strategy separate from Corporates.
   Elliott files arbitrage under hedge/arbitrage.
   They belong to a different field (equity and hedge-fund research), so I mark them as out of scope for Stochastic.
5. Missing from the draft and present in the sources: asset-based lending against specialty collateral (King Street, Davidson Kempner, Fortress), sale-leasebacks (Davidson Kempner), NAV and fund-level finance (Macfarlanes, Fortress), distressed M&A and debt-for-control (Simpson Thacher, Akin), and exit financing.
6. "Convertibles and PIPEs" appear as a labeled strategy at Oaktree (Convertible Securities) and Elliott (convertible arbitrage), and as "Preferred and Equity-Linked Instruments" at Simpson Thacher.
   Keep them as one equity-linked category.
7. Dividend recapitalization is a use case, not an instrument.
   Macfarlanes treats it as a deployment avenue for lenders.
   I found no verified consumer or foodservice dividend recap in 2025 or 2026, so it has no example below.

### Final taxonomy used in Part 2

| Code | Category | Healthy or stressed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| C1 | Structured and preferred equity, holdco PIK (capital solutions) | healthy |
| C2 | Junior debt: second lien, mezzanine, delayed-draw tranches | both |
| C3 | Convertibles and equity-linked instruments, PIPEs | both |
| C4 | Refinancing, amend and extend, covenant relief and waivers | stressed-lite |
| C5 | Liability management exercises (uptier, drop-down, exchanges) | stressed |
| C6 | Rescue, bridge, DIP and exit financing | distressed |
| C7 | Distressed M&A, loan-to-own, post-reorganization equity | distressed |
| C8 | Carve-outs and spin-off financing | healthy |
| C9 | Asset-based financing on unusual collateral (GPUs, data centers) | healthy |
| C10 | Event-driven equity: merger arbitrage, activism, spin-off investing | investor-only, not financing |

### C2 Junior debt: second lien, mezzanine, delayed-draw tranches

- Example A: TEAM, Inc. (industrial services), March 12, 2025.
  Second Amended and Restated Second Lien Term Loan Credit Agreement with Corre Partners affiliates, Cantor Fitzgerald as agent.
  About $107.4M: $97.4M term loan plus $10.0M delayed-draw tranche available to April 15, 2026 if pro forma liquidity is no more than $20M.
  Matures June 10, 2030.
  Interest 13.5%, stepping up 0.25% per quarter to a 14.5% cap.
  Interest is all PIK at first lien net leverage of 3.50x or higher, 50/50 cash and PIK between 3.00x and 3.50x, and all cash below 3.00x.
  Maximum first lien net leverage of 6.00x, tested quarterly.
  The same filing amends the ABL to permit new first lien debt and intercreditor liens.
  verified (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/318833/000119312525053470/d935596d8k.htm).
- Example B: Lifetime Brands (consumer housewares), announced August 17, 2026.
  New $60M second lien term loan from Pathlight Capital replaces the Term Loan B.
  The $200M ABL (JPMorgan agent) was amended and extended.
  Both mature August 2031.
  No warrants mentioned.
  verified (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/874396/000087439626000046/ex99108172026.htm). Self-reported.
- Why it matters: Example A gives a leverage-linked PIK/cash switch, which couples directly to a Monte Carlo of EBITDA.
  Inferred.
- Agent role: read basket and lien capacity in the first lien and ABL, size the junior tranche inside the capacity map, model the PIK/cash schedule under stress, flag intercreditor limits, draft the amendment checklist.
  Inferred.

### C3 Convertibles and equity-linked instruments, PIPEs

- Example A: Papa John's International and Starboard Value, closed February 4, 2019.

- Example B: Local Bounti (indoor agriculture), August 4, 2025.
  $10M convertible note from an existing strategic investor, 5-year maturity, 6.0% PIK interest, plus a warrant for up to 550,000 shares at $0.125.

- Example A: Denny's Corporation, Second Amendment to the Fourth Amended and Restated Credit Agreement, October 28, 2025.

- Example B: Good Times Restaurants, Third Amendment to the Amended and Restated Credit Agreement (originally April 20, 2023), event date September 30, 2025.
  Cadence Bank is agent and lender.
  Consents to closing two Bad Daddy's restaurants and ending the leases, revises the definitions of Applicable Rate, Consolidated EBITDA and Consolidated Fixed Charge Coverage Ratio, and amends the leverage covenant (Section 7.11(a)) and restricted payments (Section 7.06).

## Part 4 - Ranking for a 36-hour demo built on real public documents

1. C2 Junior debt layered under an existing credit agreement.
   TEAM, Inc. is a real, current example with a public 8-K whose exhibits include a second lien, a first lien, and an ABL amendment, so intercreditor and basket questions are real.
   The leverage-linked PIK/cash split is a ready hook for Monte Carlo.
2. C4 Amend and extend and covenant relief.
   Denny's and Good Times give a base credit agreement plus a dated amendment on EDGAR, so "what changed and does the plan still fit" is checkable.
   The autonomous moment (a clause blocks the plan, so replan) maps directly.
3. C1 Pref and structured equity.
   It is the pitch's narrative core, but should be an output option inside the structure search, not a separate document set.
4. C3 Convertibles, with Papa John's as a clean document trail.
5. C6 Rescue and DIP, strong for liquidity simulation, but court-driven.
6. C5 Liability management, strong clause reading but adversarial and legally unsettled.
7. C8 Carve-outs, large caps.
8. C7 Distressed M&A.
9. C9 Asset-heavy, great story, weak documents.
10. C10 Event-driven, not financing.

Best two for the demo: C2 and C4.
Recommended framing, inferred: a mid-size company with an existing credit agreement wants a junior layer, the agents find that a debt or lien limit blocks it, and the revised plan includes an amendment request plus the junior tranche.
That uses C4 to fix the blocker and C2 to fill the gap, and shows C1 as one candidate output.
