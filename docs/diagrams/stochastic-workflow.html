WHEN AN AGENT ASKS

  • E1Missing document. The existing agreement or an amendment is not in the upload. The agent requests it and pauses that part.
  • E2Facts to rely on. One screen of the facts that drive the plan. The reviewer confirms or corrects them.
  • E3Low-confidence clause. The quote is unclear or a defined term cannot be resolved. The agent asks and does not guess.
  • E4Plan is blocked. A clause stops the first plan. The agent shows the redesign, the consent or waiver route, and the price of the clause.
  • E5Recommendation flips. A rate move or lender change changes the best plan. The reviewer sees what changed and why.
  • E6Final approval. The reviewer approves, rejects, or asks for changes. A rejection becomes a proposed policy.

PROOF FOR JUDGES

  • Real deal, answer key. Replay a real public deal (Organogenesis, Nov 2024) from the documents that existed before it. Compare with what actually happened.
  • Catch-rate score. Planted and real clauses with recall, precision, and silent-error counts.
  • Deterministic math. 14 closed-form tests for the simulator. No number reaches a memo without a run id.
  • Limits in code. The proposing agent cannot approve its own plan. Only the gateway posts.
  • Adversarial tests. Self-approval, a forged amendment, a prompt injected inside the loan agreement, a swapped plan after approval, a poisoned policy.
  • Honest framing. A reconstruction from public filings, not the lenders' internal views.
